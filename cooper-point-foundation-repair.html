Cooper Point foundation repair

Cooper Point is a narrow peninsula between Eld Inlet and Budd Inlet. Steep lots, water moving through the bank and salt air drive most of the foundation work here.

Ground conditions in Cooper Point

Cooper Point is a narrow peninsula with saltwater on both sides, and the foundation issues here are driven by that geometry more than by anything else. Lots running down toward Eld Inlet and Budd Inlet are frequently steep, and many were developed with substantial cut-and-fill or with structures stepped down the bank on posts and piers.

Two conditions come up repeatedly. The first is bluff drainage: water moving laterally through the bank emerges at the foundation of houses built partway down the slope, so you get a persistently wet crawl space on the uphill side with no obvious surface source. The second is the marine exposure itself, salt air accelerates corrosion of anything ferrous in an unsealed crawl space, which matters when you are specifying pier coatings, brackets, duct straps and fasteners. Hot-dip galvanizing is a reasonable baseline here rather than an upsell.

Steep shoreline parcels on the point are also more likely to sit inside mapped geologically hazardous areas, which adds a layer of county review to permitted structural work and should be factored into both the schedule and the budget from the start.

Questions from Cooper Point homeowners

Why is my crawl space wet when there is no water on the surface?

On steep lots, water moves sideways through the bank and comes out at the foundation of houses built partway down the slope. The fix starts uphill of the house.

Does salt air matter under the house?

It can. Salt air speeds up rust on steel straps, fasteners and pier parts in an open crawl space. Ask what coating any steel will have.

Will my permit take longer on Cooper Point?

Steep shoreline lots are more likely to be in mapped critical areas, which can add county review. Plan extra time for structural work.
