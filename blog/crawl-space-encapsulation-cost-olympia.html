What crawl space encapsulation costs around Olympia

By Olympia Foundation Repair Pros · September 27, 2026

Short answer

For a typical 1,200 to 2,500 square foot Olympia house, a full encapsulation often lands somewhere between about $5,500 and $14,000. Drainage, cleanup and access move the number most.

Western Washington's wet winters keep a lot of crawl spaces damp from October to April. A vented dirt crawl space pulls in humid air, and that moisture ends up in the joists, the insulation and the smell upstairs. Encapsulation seals that space off from the ground and the outside air.

What a full job includes

  • Cleanup. Old insulation that has fallen or gotten wet, debris and any rodent mess come out first.
  • Water control. If water collects under the house, a drain and a sump come before any liner. Sealing in standing water makes things worse.
  • Liner. A reinforced vapor barrier, often 12 to 20 mil, across the ground and up the walls, taped at the seams and fastened at the top.
  • Sealing. Vents closed and gaps at the rim joist sealed.
  • Dehumidifier. Sized for the space and drained properly, set to keep humidity down through the wet season.

Price ranges

For a typical Olympia home of 1,200 to 2,500 square feet, a complete job often runs about $5,500 to $14,000. A liner-only job on dry ground costs less. A job with drainage, a sump and wood repair costs more.

What changes the quote

  • Whether you need drainage and a sump
  • How easy it is to get in and move around
  • How much cleanup and wood repair turns up once the old insulation is out
  • Liner thickness and whether the walls are covered too

Ask how surprises are handled. A set price for extra work, agreed before the job starts, beats an open-ended allowance. See how we approach encapsulation.

Will it lower my energy bill?

It can help, mostly by keeping floors warmer and the air drier. Savings vary a lot by house, so be wary of anyone promising a fixed percentage.

More questions

Should crawl space vents be sealed?

In a sealed, dehumidified crawl space, yes. In western Washington, open vents tend to let damp air in during much of the year. Vents should only be closed as part of a full system with moisture control.

How thick should the vapor barrier be?

Thin 6 mil plastic tears easily when people crawl on it. A reinforced liner of about 12 to 20 mil holds up much better where anyone will be moving around.
