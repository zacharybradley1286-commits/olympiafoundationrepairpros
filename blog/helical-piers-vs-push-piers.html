Helical piers or push piers: which fits your house?

By Olympia Foundation Repair Pros · September 27, 2026

Short answer

Push piers use the weight of the house to drive steel pipe down to firm soil, so they suit heavier homes. Helical piers screw into the ground, so they suit lighter structures.

Both systems carry the weight of a settled footing down past soft soil to firmer ground. They get there in different ways, and the right one depends mostly on how heavy the house is.

Push piers

A steel bracket is fastened to the footing. Hydraulic rams then press sections of steel pipe into the ground, using the weight of the house as the counterweight, until the pipe reaches firm soil or rock. Because the house supplies the force, push piers suit heavier buildings: two-story homes, masonry and full basements. The pressure it takes to drive each pier also gives a rough check of its capacity.

Helical piers

A helical pier is a steel shaft with screw plates near the tip. A hydraulic motor turns it into the ground like a large screw. Installers watch the torque as it goes down, because torque relates to how firm the soil is. Helical piers suit lighter structures that don't have enough weight to drive a push pier: one-story homes, additions, porches, decks and post-and-pier houses.

What Thurston County soil means

Much of the county sits on dense glacial till under a layer of sand, silt or fill. Piers here commonly reach firm soil somewhere around 12 to 30 feet down. The till plateau around Lacey tends to be shallower. Filled or river-deposited ground near Budd Inlet and the Deschutes tends to be deeper, and that adds pipe and cost.

A reasonable rule: heavy existing house, lean push; light structure, lean helical. When both would work, access and price usually decide. More on pier repairs.

Can helical piers go in under a house?

Often, yes. Compact equipment can install them in crawl spaces and basements with limited headroom.

More questions

How deep do piers go around Olympia?

Commonly about 12 to 30 feet, and deeper on filled or soft ground near the water. Each pier goes down until it hits the pressure or torque it was designed for.

Will pier work crack my pipes?

The piers are pressed or screwed in, not hammered, so vibration is low. Lifting the house can stress plumbing and finishes, which is why lifts are done slowly and measured as they go.
