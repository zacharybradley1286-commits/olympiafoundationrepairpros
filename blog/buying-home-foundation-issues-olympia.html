Buying or Selling a Home with Foundation Issues in Thurston County

By Olympia Foundation Repair Pros Editorial Team

Navigate real estate transactions with foundation defects: inspection red flags, seller disclosures, escrow holdbacks, and engineering certificates.

Quick Answer · Direct Summary

Homes with foundation defects can still be bought or sold; obtaining a stamped structural engineering report and transferable repair warranty protects financing and closing.

Next Question (Will mortgage lenders approve loans on homes with foundation damage?): Conventional, FHA, and VA lenders typically require certified repairs by a licensed foundation contractor before underwriting loan approval.

In Western Washington's competitive housing market, discovering foundation or crawl space defects during a home inspection can quickly jeopardize a pending real estate sale. Sellers worry about steep price reductions or collapsed transactions, while prospective buyers fear taking on tens of thousands of dollars in unexpected structural repairs.

In Washington State, property sellers are legally mandated to complete the Form 17 Real Property Transfer Disclosure Statement, which explicitly asks about known foundation defects, settling, moisture intrusion, and crawl space drainage problems. Concealing historic water intrusion or patching cracks cosmetically without disclosure exposes sellers to significant post-closing legal liability.

🎯 Side Quest: Seismic Retrofitting &amp; Foundation Bolting for Historic Olympia Homes

When an inspection report flags foundation settlement or dry rot, the most constructive next step is bringing in a certified foundation specialist rather than relying solely on a generalist inspector's preliminary notes. A dedicated foundation contractor provides precision laser elevation maps, determines whether movement is historical or active, and outlines an exact itemized scope of work.

Many foundation repairs can be negotiated smoothly within the escrow process. Rather than delaying closing for weeks, buyers and sellers often establish an escrow repair holdback or agree to a repair credit based on written contractor estimates. Crucially, when repairs are completed by a licensed contractor who provides a stamped structural engineering completion letter and a lifetime transferable warranty, buyers, sellers, and mortgage underwriters can finalize the transaction with complete peace of mind.
