Stopping mold and wood rot in a Western Washington crawl space

By Olympia Foundation Repair Pros · September 27, 2026

Short answer

Rot needs wet wood. Keep crawl space humidity down with a sealed liner, water control and a dehumidifier, and repair framing that has already gone soft.

Mild temperatures, wet ground and months of rain make crawl spaces around Olympia a good place for mold and wood rot. Surface mold on joists is common. Rot is the one that costs money, because it weakens the wood.

Mold or rot?

Push a screwdriver into discolored wood. If it goes in easily or the wood feels spongy, that is decay. Brown rot also cracks wood into small cubes. Surface mold wipes or sands off and the wood underneath is firm.

Dry it out

Spraying beams with cleaners does little if the air under the house stays damp. The fix is moisture control: a sealed liner over the ground, closed vents, water drained away where it collects, and a dehumidifier to keep humidity down through the wet season.

Repair what's damaged

Where framing has gone soft, carpenters support the floor with temporary jacks, cut out the bad sections and add new treated lumber alongside or in place of the old, fastened with bolts or structural screws. That work should happen before anything gets sealed behind a liner.

Does rot stop when the wood dries?

Decay fungi need wood that stays wet, roughly above 20 percent moisture content. Drying the wood stops active decay, but soft wood does not get its strength back.

More questions

How do I tell rot from surface mold?

Probe the wood with a screwdriver. Soft, spongy wood that the tip sinks into is rot. Firm wood with staining is usually surface mold.

Does crawl space mold affect the air upstairs?

Some air from the crawl space rises into the house, so a moldy crawl space can add odors and spores. How much depends on the house and the season.
