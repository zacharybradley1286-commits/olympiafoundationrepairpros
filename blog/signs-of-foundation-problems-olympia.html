Seven signs your Olympia house may be settling

By Olympia Foundation Repair Pros · September 27, 2026

Short answer

The signs that matter most are diagonal cracks at door and window corners, doors that stop latching, sloping floors, stair-step cracks in brick or block, and cracks that keep getting wider.

Houses around Olympia sit on a patchwork of glacial till, outwash sand, fill and, in low spots, peat. Some movement is normal in the first years after a house is built. Movement that keeps going is the problem. Here is what to look for.

  1. Diagonal cracks at door and window corners. Drywall cracks that run up and out from the top corners of openings are the most common early sign.
  2. Doors and windows that stick or won't latch. Especially if they worked fine last year.
  3. Sloping or bouncy floors. Roll a marble, or use a long level. A bouncy floor often points at the crawl space framing rather than the footing.
  4. Stair-step cracks in brick or block. Cracks that follow the mortar joints usually mean one part of the foundation dropped.
  5. Horizontal cracks in a basement wall. These point at soil pushing on the wall and should be looked at soon.
  6. A chimney pulling away. A gap between the chimney and the siding, widest at the top, is a classic sign.
  7. Gaps at trim and cabinets. Baseboards pulling off the floor or cabinets pulling away from the wall.

How to tell if it is still moving

Mark each end of a crack with a pencil line and the date, then check again after a wet month. Take a photo with a tape measure in the frame. Movement over time tells you far more than one measurement.

Musty smells and damp insulation are usually a moisture problem rather than settlement. Our crawl space page covers that.

How wide is a worrying crack?

A crack wider than about 1/8 inch, or one where one side sits higher than the other, is worth having someone look at.

More questions

Are hairline cracks normal in a newer house?

Thin vertical cracks under about 1/16 inch are common as concrete cures. Cracks that widen, leak or run diagonally are different and worth checking.

Can settling affect the roof?

It can. When part of the house drops, you may see a sagging ridge line or gutters that no longer drain the right way.
