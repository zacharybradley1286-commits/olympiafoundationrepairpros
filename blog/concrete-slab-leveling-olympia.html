Level the slab or replace it?

By Olympia Foundation Repair Pros · September 27, 2026

Short answer

If the slab is still in one piece and the cause of the sinking is fixed, foam leveling usually costs much less than tearing it out. Badly broken concrete is usually better replaced.

Sunken driveway panels, walks and patios are common around Olympia. When the soil under a slab settles or washes out, the concrete drops, leaves a trip edge and often tilts rain back toward the house.

Replacing the concrete

Tearing out and pouring new concrete takes heavy equipment and time, and new concrete needs weeks to reach full strength. If the soil underneath was the problem and nobody fixes it, the new slab can sink too.

Foam leveling

With foam leveling, the crew drills small holes, about 5/8 inch, through the slab and injects expanding polyurethane foam underneath. The foam fills the gaps and lifts the slab back up while someone watches a level. The holes get patched afterward. Foam is light, so it adds very little weight to soft soil. Most foams set fast, and a lifted driveway is usually ready for cars the same day. Ask your installer for the cure time of the product they use.

When foam is the wrong choice

  • Concrete that is crumbling or broken into many small pieces
  • Thin slabs that will crack under a point lift
  • A leak or a downspout that is still washing out the soil

Fix the cause first

Slabs here rarely sink for no reason. Downspouts that dump at the edge of the driveway, overflowing gutters, sprinklers and old utility trenches are the usual suspects. Redirect the water, then lift. More on slab leveling.

How long does foam leveling last?

Closed-cell foam does not wash out like cement slurry. The lift holds as long as the soil under it stays put, which is why fixing the water problem matters.

More questions

Can a cracked slab still be leveled?

Yes, if the pieces are large and solid. After the lift, joints and cracks are sealed with a flexible sealant to keep water out.

Is the foam safe around gardens?

Once cured, the foams used for lifting are inert. Ask for the product data sheet if you want the details for a specific product.
