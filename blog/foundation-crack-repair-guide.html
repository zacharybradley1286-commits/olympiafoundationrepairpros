Horizontal or vertical: which foundation cracks to worry about

By Olympia Foundation Repair Pros · September 27, 2026

Short answer

Thin vertical cracks are often from concrete shrinking as it cured. Stair-step cracks suggest settlement. Horizontal cracks and bowing walls point at soil pushing in and need attention soonest.

Finding a crack in your foundation is unsettling, but many cracks are harmless. Direction, width and whether one side has shifted tell you most of what you need to know.

Vertical cracks

Straight up-and-down cracks in a poured wall are the most common kind in Olympia basements and crawl spaces. Most are shrinkage from when the concrete cured. If a vertical crack is thinner than about 1/8 inch and both sides are flush, it is usually a leak concern rather than a structural one. Injection seals it.

Stair-step and diagonal cracks

Cracks that step along mortar joints in block or brick, or run diagonally from a corner, usually mean one part of the footing has dropped relative to the rest. The fix is usually support under that part of the footing, often with steel piers.

Horizontal cracks

A crack running level along a basement wall, especially with the wall leaning in, means wet soil is pushing on it. Left alone, the wall can keep moving. Repairs include carbon fiber straps for walls that have moved only a little, and steel braces or wall anchors for walls that have moved more. An engineer should help decide which.

Watch it

Mark the ends of a crack and the date, photograph it with a ruler, and check after a wet month. Growth is the most useful thing you can report when you call.

Can cracks be sealed from inside?

Leaking, non-structural cracks can be sealed from inside with epoxy or polyurethane injection. Walls that are bowing need bracing, not just sealing.

More questions

How does crack injection work?

Small ports are set along the crack and a resin is pumped in until it fills the crack through the wall. Polyurethane is common for leaks; epoxy is used where some bonding strength is wanted.

Are carbon fiber straps a permanent fix?

They can stop further movement on walls that have bowed only slightly. Walls that have moved more usually need steel braces or anchors. An engineer should make that call.
