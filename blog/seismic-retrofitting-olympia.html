Seismic retrofits for older Olympia homes

By Olympia Foundation Repair Pros · September 27, 2026

Short answer

A basic retrofit bolts the wood sill to the concrete foundation and braces short cripple walls with plywood, so the house is less likely to slide or tip off its foundation.

Western Washington sits above the Cascadia Subduction Zone and has shallow faults under Puget Sound. Olympia's older neighborhoods, like South Capitol, Bigelow Highlands and the Eastside, have many homes built before modern seismic rules.

The weak spot

In many older houses the wood sill plate just sits on the concrete. Weight and friction hold it there. In strong shaking the house can slide off the foundation, which can break gas and water lines.

Bolting

The first step is bolting the sill to the foundation. Installers drill through the sill into the concrete and set anchor bolts. Where there isn't room to drill from above, steel plates are fastened to the side of the sill and the foundation instead.

Bracing cripple walls

Some houses have short stud walls between the foundation and the first floor. These cripple walls can fold sideways in an earthquake. Nailing structural plywood to them, with the right nail pattern and metal connectors, stiffens them.

Before you start

A retrofit may need a permit. Most structural foundation work, such as piering or bracing a wall, needs a building permit from the city you live in (Olympia, Lacey or Tumwater) or from Thurston County if you are outside city limits. The permit office may also ask for a design or letter from a licensed engineer. Smaller, non-structural jobs may not need a permit; the permit office makes that call. If you want the work considered for insurance, ask your insurer what paperwork they need before work begins.

How do I know if my house is bolted?

Look in the crawl space along the top of the foundation wall. You should see bolt heads with nuts and washers through the wood sill, or steel plates on its side, every few feet.

More questions

How long does a retrofit take?

For a typical single-family home, bolting and bracing often takes a few days. Tight crawl spaces and extra plumbing or ductwork add time.

Can a retrofit lower my insurance cost?

Some insurers consider retrofits when pricing earthquake coverage. Ask your insurer before the work starts so you get the right documents.
