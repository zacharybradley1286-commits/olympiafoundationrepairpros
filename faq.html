Foundation repair questions, answered

Plain answers about cracks, settling, crawl spaces, permits, cost and selling a house with foundation work.

Inspections and estimates

Do you inspect before giving a price?

Yes. We look at the house, including the crawl space or basement and the drainage outside, and then write an estimate. We cover Olympia, Lacey, Tumwater and nearby Thurston County.

Why do quotes for the same house vary so much?

Usually because they are quoting different work. Check whether each quote includes engineering, permits, digging and cleanup, how many piers it covers, whether the per-pier price changes with depth, and what the warranty covers. Compare the scope before the total.

What does foundation repair cost around here?

It varies a lot. Sealing a crack or lifting one slab often lands in the low thousands. Pier work is priced per pier and whole jobs often run well into five figures. Depth to firm soil, pier count, access and permits move the number most. Our guide to repair costs has ranges.

Is financing available?

Ask when we give you the estimate. If you compare financing offers, look at the full cost over the loan, not just the monthly payment.

Figuring out the problem

What are the first signs of settlement?

Diagonal cracks running up from door and window corners, doors that stick or won't latch, sloping or bouncy floors, stair-step cracks in brick or block, and trim pulling away from walls. A musty crawl space usually points at moisture rather than settlement.

Which cracks are serious?

Thin vertical cracks are often shrinkage from when the concrete cured. Stair-step cracks suggest one part of the footing dropped. Horizontal cracks and walls leaning inward mean soil is pushing on the wall and should be looked at soonest. A crack that keeps growing matters more than a wide one that has been stable for years.

How do I tell if a crack is still moving?

Mark each end with a pencil line and the date and take a photo with a ruler in it. Check again after a wet month or two. Change over time tells you more than one measurement.

Is it settlement or just an old house?

Some settling in the first years after a house is built is normal and then stops. What matters is whether it is still moving and whether one part is dropping more than another. Floor measurements taken a few months apart show the trend.

Do I need a structural engineer too?

For anything beyond sealing a crack, it is often worth it, and permits for structural work may require one anyway. An engineer's assessment gives you an independent view before a big repair.

During the work

How long does the work take?

Pier jobs usually take a few days on site once the permit is in hand. Crawl space encapsulation often takes one to three days, depending on size and drainage. Slab lifting is often done in a few hours. Waiting on the permit is usually the longest part.

Do repairs need a permit?

Most structural foundation work, such as piering or bracing a wall, needs a building permit from the city you live in (Olympia, Lacey or Tumwater) or from Thurston County if you are outside city limits. The permit office may also ask for a design or letter from a licensed engineer. Smaller, non-structural jobs may not need a permit; the permit office makes that call. We confirm what your job needs before we put it on the schedule.

Will my landscaping survive?

Pier work means small pits dug right against the footing, so plants next to the foundation will be disturbed. Beds a few feet out usually survive. Exterior drainage means digging around the house, so plants, walks or decks in the way are affected. Ask what gets put back.

Do I need to move out?

Rarely. Most work happens outside or in the crawl space. Expect noise and equipment in the yard.

Will the repair close my drywall cracks?

Sometimes partly. Lifting can close cracks that opened as the house dropped, but repairing drywall, paint, tile and trim is usually separate work. Settle who handles it before work starts.

How much can the house be lifted?

Stopping the movement is the goal we can commit to. Some lift is often possible and doors and windows often work better afterward, but pushing a house that settled decades ago all the way back can crack finishes upstairs. Be wary of anyone promising the house will go back exactly level.

Warranties, insurance and selling

What does your warranty cover?

Terms depend on the repair. The warranty for your job is written into your estimate so you can read it before you decide.

What should I check in any foundation warranty?

Read the document itself, not the word "lifetime" on a brochure. Check whether it covers stopping movement only or lift too, whether it is the contractor's or the product maker's, whether it transfers to a buyer and how, and what voids it.

Does homeowners insurance cover it?

Usually not for settlement or soil movement, which most policies exclude. Damage from a sudden event such as a burst pipe is more likely to be covered. Ask your insurer directly.

Will a foundation repair hurt my sale?

An unrepaired, known problem usually hurts more. Washington sellers generally list known problems on the Form 17 seller statement, and buyers and lenders look more kindly on a finished, permitted repair with paperwork. See our buying and selling guide.

Should I repair before listing or give the buyer a credit?

Both are common. Repairing first removes a question mark but costs cash up front. A credit based on written estimates keeps your cash but can reopen negotiations. Either way, a real engineering assessment keeps the number grounded.
