Lacey foundation repair

Lacey and Hawks Prairie mostly sit on the firmer till plateau east of I-5. Here, the problems are more often water and sunken slabs than deep settlement.

Ground conditions in Lacey

Lacey sits largely on the Vashon till plateau east of I-5, generally firmer, better-draining ground than the shoreline neighborhoods west of the capitol. That has a practical consequence: where piering is needed here, competent bearing is often reached at the shallower end of the county's range, which usually means fewer pier sections and a lower per-pier cost than a comparable job on filled ground near the inlet.

The most common problems in Lacey are less about deep settlement and more about water. Much of the housing stock is post-1980 subdivision construction on engineered fill, built fast across Hawks Prairie, Meridian and the Willamette Drive corridor. Where that fill was placed over the prairie's gravelly outwash, later utility trenching and poorly routed downspouts can wash fines out and leave voids under slabs, patios and garage floors, which shows up as a sinking driveway panel or a garage slab pulling away from the stem wall rather than as a cracked foundation wall.

The other recurring issue is the shallow perched water table across the prairie in a wet winter, which puts crawl spaces under sustained humidity from November onward.

Questions from Lacey homeowners

Why is my Lacey driveway sinking?

Water from downspouts, gutters or old utility trenches can wash fine soil out from under a slab and leave a void. Fixing the water and then lifting the slab usually solves it.

Are piers cheaper in Lacey?

Firm soil is often shallower on the Lacey plateau, which can mean shorter piers than on filled ground near the inlet. Every lot is different, though.

Why is my crawl space damp every winter?

Parts of the prairie have a shallow water table in wet winters. Drainage usually matters more than liner thickness here.
