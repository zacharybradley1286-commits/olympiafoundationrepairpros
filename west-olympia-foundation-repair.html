West Olympia foundation repair

West Olympia climbs from Capitol Lake and Budd Inlet toward Division and Harrison. Slopes and cut-and-fill building pads shape most of the foundation problems here.

Ground conditions in West Olympia

West Olympia covers the ground rising away from Capitol Lake and Budd Inlet toward Division and Harrison, and slope is the defining variable. Many lots here were cut and filled to create a building pad, which leaves part of the foundation bearing on undisturbed till and part on fill of uncertain compaction. Differential settlement follows that line almost exactly, which is why cracking on these houses so often runs diagonally across one corner rather than uniformly around the structure.

Slope also changes where water goes. On a hillside lot, groundwater moves downhill through the soil profile and arrives at the uphill foundation wall under pressure, rather than soaking straight down as it would on flat ground. Daylight basements here commonly show water at the uphill wall and nothing at all on the downhill side.

The practical upshot is that a proper diagnosis in West Olympia has to establish whether you have a bearing problem, a water problem, or both, because piering a wall that is actually being pushed by saturated hillside backfill does not solve it.

Questions from West Olympia homeowners

Why do West Olympia houses crack across one corner?

Many lots were cut into the hill and filled to make a flat pad. Part of the house ends up on firm till and part on fill, and the fill side settles. Cracks tend to follow that line.

My daylight basement leaks on the uphill side only. Why?

On a slope, groundwater moves downhill through the soil and reaches the uphill wall under pressure. The downhill side often stays dry. The fix is usually drainage on the uphill side.

Do hillside homes always need piers?

No. A wall pushed by wet hillside soil needs drainage or bracing, not piers. Piers help when part of the footing has actually dropped.
